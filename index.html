About Me

I am in my final year of BScHons Actuarial Science at Wits with eight ASSA exemptions. Actuarial science pulled me in because the problems are real: the maths underpins decisions about whether people can retire comfortably, access healthcare, or protect their families. That is the kind of work I want to be doing.

Outside of coursework I tutor over 120 second-year students across financial mathematics, business applications, and life contingencies. It has turned out to be one of the most rewarding parts of my week. Explaining an idea until it genuinely clicks for someone is a different skill from being able to derive it yourself, and doing it every week has made me a clearer thinker and a better communicator.

I am genuinely excited about what AI tools can do for actuaries, and I do not think the profession should be threatened by them. Used well, AI extends what one person can do and frees up time for the kind of judgement that models cannot replicate. I aspire to be an actuary who utilises these tools properly, effectively and efficiently.

Honours Research Project · 2026

Mortality Forecasting: RNNs vs Lee-Carter

Constructed and evaluated Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) recurrent neural network architectures for long-horizon mortality forecasting, benchmarked against the Lee-Carter model on a 10-year test set. England and Wales data formed the primary dataset, with Japanese female mortality data sourced from the Human Mortality Database (HMD) providing a key exception case in the analysis.

Key Findings
  • Lee-Carter substantially outperformed both RNN architectures overall
  • RNNs failed to capture mortality improvement trends over the long horizon
  • LSTM outperformed Lee-Carter for Japanese female mortality (Human Mortality Database)
  • GRU generally outperformed LSTM despite having fewer parameters

Retirement Savings Analyser

MiWay Actuarial Hackathon · 2026

Premium Pricing in a Competitive Market

Modelled the risk cost and office premium for 500,000 policies across a simulated multi-year competitive insurance market. Built Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) models, split into frequency and severity components, to capture the interactions between rating factors and inform a premium loading strategy. Placed 3rd out of the competing teams.
